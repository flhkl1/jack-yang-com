If I were to pick the soundtrack to perfectly capture the spirit of my senior year, these 8 songs would be no-brainers.

**Blue Monday - New Order (1988)**

What better song to capture the monotony and exhaustion of senior year than *Blue Monday*? I’ve found the rhythmic, ruthless kick drum pattern that echoes alone throughout the intro to be oddly tranquilising. Singer Bernard Summer interrupts 2 minutes of robotic instrumentals with a verse that oozes sheer resentment (which is oddly cathartic when I’m stressed). The end of the song features a dizzying onslaught of synths that perfectly summarises how overwhelming senior year can feel sometimes.

**Don’t You Worry Child - Swedish House Mafia (2012)**

When moments of despondency hit me in senior year, *Don’t You Worry Child* is the first thing blasting through my headphones. With its reminiscences of better times as a “king” in a “happy home”, acknowledgements of loss and heartbreak, and its raucous chorus preaching reassurance, recovery and triumph, the song has always felt like a distillation of accepting your situation and moving on, with an undying faith that things will get better.

**On Sight - Kanye West (2013)**

*On Sight,* with its cacophonous opening and continuously erratic beat, is a jarring splash of ice water that shocks me out of bed in the morning. It’s fast paced, completely weird, and I can’t get enough of it. West’s decision to insert a heavily spiritual choir in between two overconfident and discordant verses perfectly sums up the journey of finding your identity in senior year; everyone will encounter moments of vulnerability and confusion, but the underlying desire to forge your own identity will always resurface.

**Imma Be - Black Eyed Peas (2010)**

As the name of this magazine implies, motivation in senior year comes and goes. When it’s here, *Imma Be* may very well be the anthem to my ambitions. Its recurring motif, a short tune that first plays at the beginning of the song, is success and extravagance in sound form. By the beat switch 2 and a half minutes in, I’m either zoned in and completely focused on whatever work I’m trying to do, or vigorously nodding my head to the beat, my work forgotten and my mind completely devoted to the most nostalgic band of the 2000s. There isn’t an in-between.

**Last Call - Kanye West (2004)**

Senior year, with its ceremonious finality and the high-stakes university application process, may seem like the be-all, end-all. JAY-Z’s derisive shriek of laughter in the opening of *Last Call* buries this idea 6 feet under. Throughout the song, West raps about his struggles as a former producer failing to get noticed as an aspiring lyricist. To me, the song is a reminder of the importance of continuous perseverance, at a time when finishing high school legitimately seems like the only thing on the horizon. Despite West’s narrative of rejection and perseverance spanning almost 13 minutes, the whole song feels like an upbeat, premature celebration of yourself, which is why it’s so charming and oddly comforting.

**Senior Skip Day - Mac Miller (2012)**

Is it possible to feel nostalgia for something that hasn’t even happened yet? If your first instinct is to dismiss this question, listen to *Senior Skip Day*. Just as nostalgia forces us to idealise events that have happened in the past, this song, with its feel-good tunes and mischievous lyrics, takes me far away to a senior year devoid of stress and pressure. Miller’s urge to “enjoy the best things in your life, cause you ain’t gonna get to live it twice” particularly resonates with me. It’s a playful yet solemn reminder that, amidst seemingly never ending deadlines and tasks, some valuable time off with friends and family is still important.

**Baby Blue - Badfinger (1971)**

*“Guess I got what I deserve, kept you waiting for too long my love.”* Behind the upbeat, almost triumphant guitar chords that explode from the outset of *Baby Blue*, there’s a definite twinge of melancholy and regret that hit me pretty hard when I first listened to it. I’m not just saying this because it’s the last song on *Breaking Bad;* there’s an air of finality to this song that encapsulates the bittersweetness of senior year for me, simultaneously bringing back my biggest regrets while filling my mind with gratitude and acceptance.

**SMUCKERS - Tyler, the Creator (2018)**

*SMUCKERS* is such a good song. It’s funny *(“Richer than white people with black kids”)*, embodies overconfidence *("I'M THE ONLY \*\*\*\*\* TO EVER CHECK NIKE")* and the wordplay is amazing *("I'm starin at a tramp on lean, make my eye jump")*. Also, literally everyone on this song kills their verses. I don’t really associate *SMUCKERS* with any particular moment in senior year, but when I need a laugh, and a really good song, I’ll always go back to it.
