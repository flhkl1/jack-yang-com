This is a memo I wrote to my group members in EECS 182 and CS 180. Paper results to follow.

Academic research is often obfuscated but the underlying goal should be solving high-leverage problems. High-leverage is relat
ive and obviously a matter of personal taste but as a 22-year-old newcomer to the field I'll approximate leverage as  maximal benefit to the maximum amount of people.

Practically we are in a class that expects a delieverable in a month and a half. Some heuristics, and some approximation of an algorithm to come up with an idea:

1. Pick a promising field. What that means is that, with reasonable effort, someone could reason about from first principles that it would become very impactful in the future, while also being small enough currently that no one is talking about it. This is sort of akin to picking a winning stock. Since your investment is your time and energy instead of your money, I think this search process deserves least as much due diligence. 

2. Do due diligence. This involves literature review to assess what the most effective people are doing at the frontier to solve the problems in this space. I think you can start by listening to a hunch about such problems. For example, a natural question my roommate had, given that his conviction was that self-driving cars might be pretty good, is how to make different AIs collaborate together? That led him into investigating the nascent field of multi-agent reinforcement learning around 2019, which he eventually niched down towards his first published paper on using variance reduction to show multi-agent credit assignment.

3. Execute. This is probably the hardest part and the part for which I, at this current time, don't have the best notes on. Hopefully I can get back to this.

A few other notes I made, in conversation with my roommate about research:

- Pick regular meetings with an advisor

- Listen to them when they say that you are doing something incorrectly, without being attached to your position

- Never rest on your laurels and always think as far as you reasonably can into the future when you choose research topics to ivnestigate

- Read more research papers.
